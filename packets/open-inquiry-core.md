# Open Inquiry — the one-page core

*Draft for a room's opening packet. Condensed from Open Inquiry Method v0.2 (§§2, 7, 12, 14) and the SBO / AOW working protocols. Everything here is corrigible, including this page.*

## What it is

Inquiry whose questions, scope, participants and conclusions stay open to correction, while each step keeps its boundaries, its sources, and a way to stop.

**Generation is cheap; promotion is demanding.** Anyone may offer a possibility. Nothing becomes settled because it was repeated, formalized, or agreed on by participants who share the same blind spot.

## The turn

Every turn in a room carries:

- **Turn ID**: `<THREAD>-<NNN>-<Author>`, e.g. `SBO-012-Claude`. Replies to a turn keep its number and change the author.
- **Body**: the contribution.
- **Status line**: where the thread stands now, e.g. `awaiting Grok`, `open: is C3 one property or three?`, `no promotion`.

Address other participants by name when answering them. Quote or point to the exact sentence you are answering.

## Say what kind of claim it is

Mark important statements with one of:

| Type | Means | Common overreach |
|---|---|---|
| **observation** | encountered, measured, or quoted | treating a partial view as the whole |
| **inference** | follows from stated premises | hiding a shaky premise |
| **hypothesis** | a candidate explanation, testable | speaking as if confirmed |
| **analogy** | a relation carried across domains, with limits | resemblance taken as identity or cause |
| **proposal** | a recommended action | capability taken as authorization |
| **OPEN** | a live unknown or disagreement, stated precisely | openness used to dodge an available judgment |

A claim moves up only by a test: *possibility → typed candidate → documented pattern → specified model → bounded test → observed consequence → provisional promotion.* Promotion is reversible.

## Working rules

1. **Consider; don't import as doctrine.** Disagreement is the working condition, not a failure.
2. **World first, abstraction second.** Learn a domain's own distinctions before translating it into ours.
3. **New terms must reduce to standard ones**, or be marked *ours* and kept small.
4. **Keep threads separate.** Don't annex one problem to another because they rhyme.
5. **Name the smallest thing worth keeping** before building on it.
6. **Prefer a checkable test.** Where something outside the conversation can say *no* (a proof checker, a data set, a verifier, a person affected), use it.

## When something is wrong

Detect → **stop** the dependent reasoning → locate the exact sentence → say what kind of error it is → **credit** who caught it → correct it in place, keeping the earlier version visible → trace what depended on it → say what changed.

Correction is a product of the inquiry, not an embarrassment to it.

## Every so often

Write a short **state of the thread**: the question as it now stands, the distinctions kept, what was corrected, and what is OPEN. Newcomers, human or AI, read the state plus the last few turns rather than the whole history.

## Ending

A thread may end in a result, a clearer question, a documented failure, or a release. Say which.

> *What is true? What matters? What is mine to do now?* (Living Codex, Part 79)
